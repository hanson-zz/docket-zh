## What it is

Dockling (formerly Docket 中文版) is an **unofficial fork** of [Docket](https://github.com/nparashar150/dockset),
an MIT licensed open source macOS app. It started as a Simplified Chinese localization and has grown into its own app:

- The interface is available in **Simplified Chinese and English** and follows the system language.
  If your preferred languages include neither Chinese nor English (French, Japanese, German and so on),
  Dockling shows English; Traditional Chinese, Hong Kong and Taiwan show Simplified Chinese.
  You can also pick a language in **Settings ▸ General ▸ Language**.
- It builds for anyone who clones it: ad-hoc signing and its own bundle ID (`com.docket-zh.app`),
  so it can be installed next to upstream Docket.
- Dozens of new widgets, clock faces and weather views, shelf looks, per-widget conditions,
  shrink to fit, and three ways to switch layouts.

The basics it shares with upstream:

- **Follows the real Dock**: size, edge, magnification and auto-hide are read live from System Settings.
- **App groups**: drag one icon onto another and hold to make a group, like on iOS.
- **Layouts**: save several and write them back to Apple's Dock (only `persistent-apps` changes, with verification and rollback).
- **Click a widget to open its details panel** with more information and actions.

## Features

More than fifty widgets, grouped by use. Mix them freely on one shelf.

- **Apps, files and quick access**: app groups, folders, Downloads, File Shelf, Clipboard, Screenshots, Photo Frame, Bookmarks, App Switcher, Shortcuts, Search.
- **Productivity and focus**: Calendar, Week Agenda, Reminders, Quick To-do, Sticky Note, Focus Timer, Habits, Stand Up reminders, Anniversaries, Time Progress, Slacker Clock, AI Assistant, Translate, Codex Usage, GitHub.
- **Mac control and system**: Battery (with charge limit and battery alerts), System Activity, Network Activity, Gauges, Volume, App Volume, Microphone, Quick Toggles, Wallpaper, Trash, Mail.
- **Handy tools**: Calc (calculator, unit converter and currency in one), Color Picker, Emoji, Capture, Recorder, AirDrop.
- **Media and fun**: Now Playing (Apple Music, browser tabs, NetEase Cloud Music, Xiaoyuzhou), Radio, Podcasts, Ambient Sound, Virtual Pet, the Red Panda's Basket, Water.
- **Time, weather and markets**: Clock and World Clock, Weather and forecasts, Lunar & Holidays, Stocks, Watchlist, Crypto.
- **Live Alerts**: a small capsule below the notch for volume, brightness and upcoming meetings.
- **Dockling Pro**: a one-time purchase that unlocks extra widgets and looks. The basics are always free.

## In development (next version)

- **Red panda empty states with actions**: Habits, Anniversaries, Alarms, Radio, Podcasts, Bookmarks, Screenshots, Photos and an empty Basket share the existing red panda artwork, one caption and one working action. Buttons open add forms, search, subscription input, screen capture, folder selection or Add Widget. Narrow cards hide the caption and artwork before reducing to a button, without truncation. Not yet verified on a physical Mac.

- **System Now Playing for three audio sources**: Radio, Podcasts and Ambient Sound share one media session and pause the other two when starting. System controls support play, pause, toggle and stop; Radio switches stations, Podcasts navigate chapters or skip back 15 seconds and forward 30 seconds. Titles, show names, available artwork and progress appear in Now Playing. Live radio has no duration; stopping clears metadata and command handlers. Not yet verified on a physical Mac.

- **Readable offline quotes**: Stocks, Watchlist and Crypto keep offline captions at 9.5pt or larger. Narrow cards wrap or use "Offline", with the complete quote time in the tooltip and accessibility label. Weather's five Now captions and saved-style migration stay unchanged. Not yet verified on a physical Mac.

- **Time Progress and Habits are free, Watchlist gets bigger type, and Weather's seven styles become four**: Time Progress is now free: Ring, Percentage and Bars for everyone, while Liquid and the four-up grid stay Pro and are still marked Pro in Add Widget and the style menu; a Liquid or grid card already on the shelf no longer gets the lock overlay without Pro and draws as the ring instead. Habits is free for 3 habits: the fourth habit on and the 7-day report in the panel are Pro, and with 3 habits New Habit shows a lock and opens the paywall. Nothing is deleted for anyone who already has more than 3: the extra habits are dimmed with a lock in the panel and are read only (they can be viewed and deleted, not checked in, filled in, edited or moved into the first three), and when an earlier habit is deleted the next one moves up and becomes usable; on a side shelf the fourth row is dimmed and cannot be clicked too. Onboarding's Health adds Habits again; the Red Panda's Basket is still Pro, and the water, habit and focus it counts are now all free widgets. Whole Pro widgets go from 20 to 18. The wide Watchlist card goes from two columns of three to two columns of two (4 symbols), rows from 12pt to 18pt, and the symbol, price, change, the up and down count and Today are all at least 9.5pt and no longer shrink to fit; from a fifth symbol on, the last cell says "N more", and clicking the group name moves to the next group. The side shelf still shows one symbol per row. Weather goes from 7 styles to 4: Now gains a Caption option (city, conditions, wind, sunrise and sunset, or wind and sun times), Daily Forecast has a Days option (7 or 10), plus Hourly forecast and Multiple cities (still Pro). Conditions, Wind & sun times, Next 7 days and Next 10 days cards already on the shelf turn into the new style with the matching option when they load, at the same look and size. Within two hours of rain the Now style's caption says "Rain in about 40 min" first (from the hourly forecast's precipitation, rounded to 5 minutes), and goes back to the chosen caption once it is raining or the rain has passed. The Time Progress fallback, the free habit count and read only rules, the Watchlist rows and row height, the weather style migration and the minutes until rain live in Core with tests. Not yet verified on a real Mac.
- **Sticky Note writing is bolder and larger, and readable on mid-tone paper**: the handwriting on the card was Hannotate W5 at 14 pt and shrank to 0.75 when it did not fit, which left barely 10 pt on a 58 pt card, with thin strokes that blurred on slate, kraft or coral paper. It now prefers the bolder Hannotate W7 at 15 pt (12 pt in a vertical shelf) and shrinks no further than 0.9, dropping a line with an ellipsis instead; when Hannotate is not installed (it is a download-on-demand font) it falls back to the rounded system font in semibold rather than the thin default. The ink is now chosen by contrast with the paper: whichever of the dark and light ink reads better, pushed towards black or white until it reaches the WCAG body text ratio of 4.5:1, with a test for every one of the sixteen papers; the large sheet you write on in the panel uses the same ink. Not yet verified on a real Mac.
- **"Notifications are off · Turn on" next to reminder switches when macOS blocks them**: Stand-up, Water (when it is not using the pop-up card), stock price alerts, new podcast episodes, GitHub, Codex usage, Battery, makeup workday reminders and the virtual pet asked for permission once and never looked at the answer, so with Dockling's notifications turned off in System Settings the switch stayed on and nothing ever arrived. These switches now read the notification permission and, when it is off, show an orange "Notifications are off · Turn on" line that opens System Settings ▸ Notifications ▸ Dockling; nothing shows while the switch is off or permission has not been asked yet. Every place shares one view, and how the permission is classified and which switches count as notification switches live in Core with tests. Not yet verified on a real Mac.
- **Market cards say "Offline · price at 10:32" when they cannot refresh**: Stocks, Watchlist and Crypto used to fade to 55% after a failed refresh without saying why. The card's bottom line now reads "Offline · price at 10:32" (with the date when it is not today; Watchlist puts the oldest stale time at the top right, Crypto gives up its 7-day line for it), and the card only fades to 70%. The panels show the same unplugged banner as Weather and GitHub at the top, with a button to fetch again right away (Crypto skips its five-minute wait). The time text and the oldest-time rule live in Core with tests. Not yet verified on a real Mac.
- **Drop things straight onto AI Portal, Bookmarks and Sticky Note**: drag text or files onto AI Portal and a blue dashed outline reads "Ask AI about it"; dropping opens the ask panel with them in the field (plain text files bring their contents under a line with the file name, images and PDFs bring their path; when the notch chat is in web mode it goes to the clipboard to paste into the chat box). Drag a link onto Bookmarks and a teal outline reads "Add a bookmark"; dropping adds it at the end with its title from the link preview, and a duplicate or a full row of 8 beeps. Drag text onto Sticky Note and an orange outline reads "Add to the note"; dropping adds a line to the end of the front sheet. All three use the same four-colour dashed outline as File Shelf, AirDrop, Trash and Quick To-do. Composing the prompt, the duplicate check and adding a line live in Core with tests. Not yet verified on a real Mac.
- **The English interface gives new widgets regional defaults**: English used to start with Chinese defaults. New Crypto widgets now price in US dollars outside mainland China, rises are green and falls red except in mainland China, Taiwan, Japan and Korea, gold and silver default to USD/oz, and the Slacker Clock's money uses the region's symbol ($ in the US, £ in the UK, € in the euro area). The Chinese interface is unchanged, and widgets already placed keep their own settings. Calc's badge shows a plus-minus symbol instead of a Chinese character in English, and the default bookmark that was translated to a second GitHub is now YouTube. The default rules live in Core with tests. Not yet verified on a real Mac.
- **English reminder pop-up cards are no longer cut off**: in English the water line ended in "1000 / 2000…" and the hint below in "Raises the cup for 3…". The amount you have drunk is never truncated now, and "cups to go" moves to the next line when it does not fit; when the hint and buttons do not fit on one line, the hint takes its own line (up to two) with the buttons right-aligned below; the third line may wrap to two lines, and the English water and to-do hints are shorter. Not yet verified on a real Mac.
- **Water reminders move to the background timer too, and clicking a pop-up card always opens its widget's panel**: a water reminder used to be scheduled only once the Water card had been drawn on the shelf, so a card in a folder or on another layout never reminded, and Snooze 15 minutes on the pop-up card scheduled nothing. Now, like countdowns, the background timer watches every Water widget on every layout (folders included) from launch, so reminders come with the shelf auto-hidden or the card on another layout, Snooze 15 minutes is picked up within a second, and a deleted Water card no longer fires once more on its old time; it still pops up once, never alongside a system notification. Clicking the body of a pop-up card (or a stock price or stand-up notification) now: slides an auto-hidden shelf out first and grows the panel from the card when it is on the shelf; grows it from the folder's tile when the card sits in a folder on the current layout, without opening the folder; and opens it as a free-floating panel at the top centre of the screen (where the pop-up card was) when the card is on another layout, only the Apple Dock is in use, or the card is hidden by its condition, without switching layouts. Changes in that panel are written back to the widget on its own layout, and it stays put when the shelf slides away. Where the panel hangs and how each second's water time is handled live in Core with tests. Not yet verified on a real Mac.
- **Countdowns, alarms and reminders come due without their card on screen, countdowns and alarms pop a card too, and hovering a day shows the details**: countdowns, alarms, quick to-dos and reminders are now watched by one background timer in Dockling from launch, so they come due even when the shelf is auto-hidden or the widget sits in a folder or on another layout. Countdowns and alarms now also slide out the dark glass card: a countdown says "Noodles is done", when it ended and how long it ran, with Again (the same length once more) and Close; an alarm shows its name and time (a snoozed one also shows the time it was set for), with Snooze 9 minutes and Close, and snoozing on the widget and in its panel also moves from 10 to 9 minutes. The timers still chime on their own (every 4 seconds for 30 seconds on a countdown, for two minutes on an alarm, silent when the widget's sound is off); the speaker on the card names Glass or Sosumi and is struck through when the sound is off. Stopping, dismissing or snoozing on the widget tucks the card away, Esc on a countdown or alarm card means Close, and an alarm's card stays for its two minutes of ringing. Reminders are read from the system Reminders app (those due today with a time, from the lists the Reminders widgets are filtered to, only when one of them is on some layout, read again every 5 minutes, after midnight and when Reminders changes), and quick to-dos from the reminder times each to-do widget stores, so both pop up with the shelf away. Close to a due time and while something rings, Dockling asks macOS not to delay its timer, and saves power the rest of the time. Settings, Shelf, Sounds, Pop up a card when due adds Countdown and Alarm, on by default; turning one off keeps the chime and the orange card, just without the pop-up. Hovering the Calendar widget's day timeline or the Week widget no longer just swaps its title: a details card floats outside the widget with the title, the time (how long until, in progress or ended), the place or meeting service, the calendar's colour and name, and a Join meeting button while a linked meeting has not ended; the Week widget lists up to five events of that day with how busy it is. Moving the pointer onto the details keeps them up, a click on the widget tucks them away, and with Reduce motion they appear and disappear without fading. What just came due, whether it still rings, when to stay precise, which lists to read, the countdown and alarm card wording and the details card's text and placement live in Core with tests. Not yet verified on a real Mac.

After switching gold and silver between yuan per gram and dollars per ounce, alert prices you already set are converted with the exchange rate and 1 oz = 31.1035 g, keep their direction, and an alert that already fired does not fire again; if the rate has not arrived when you switch, the conversion waits for it, and until then those alerts are not compared against a price in the other unit. Quick To-do no longer deletes yesterday's finished items: they go to a collapsible "N tucked away" section at the bottom of the panel where you can view them, restore one as today's open item or delete it, and they are cleared 7 days after they were done; tucked away items are no longer marked for today and carry no reminder time. The global quick-capture box now finds a Quick To-do inside a group (one on the top level wins), and widgets inside groups no longer lose what they write. The Reminders panel adds Tomorrow and Next 7 Days below Overdue and Today, so an item pushed to tomorrow or next Monday moves to another section instead of vanishing; the notice only appears when it moves further out or loses its date, and the Reminders card on the shelf still covers today. Anniversary month abbreviations on the tear-off page and timeline are no longer hard-coded and come from the system date formats for the interface language (5月 in Chinese, May in English). With several sticky notes stacked, scrolling or swiping over the note flips forward and back (down for next, up for previous, one sheet per gesture; Command-scroll still switches layouts), and with Reduce Motion on the sheet changes at once without animation. Notification routing, alert price conversion, the tucked away list and its expiry, finding the to-do for quick capture, panel sections, month abbreviations and paging both ways are in Core with tests. Not yet verified on a real Mac.
- **The AI Portal's mini ask panel gains clipboard relay, quick actions and saved prompts, widget panels get an Appearance row, and Battery's Status style draws a battery**: the small ask panel that opens from the AI Portal card now has the same row as the notch chat under its input box: Paste brings the clipboard's text into the box (on a new line after anything already typed, up to the first 20,000 characters), the Translate, Sum up, Polish and Explain quick actions, and saved prompts under Prompts. They use the input box first and the clipboard when it is empty, and the filled-in prompt goes to the chosen service like any question: API and web modes hand it to the notch chat (still Dockling Pro, so the Pro window opens until it is unlocked), jump mode opens the service with the question where it can or copies it first. With no text on either side it asks you to type or copy something. The line below previews the clipboard and brings it in when clicked, and Manage Prompts… opens Settings, AI. Both places share one view rather than two copies. Widgets with a color or background to choose now end their panel with an Appearance row showing a dot in the theme color and the current color and background (such as “Pink · Dots background”, or Default); click it to open the same Colors and Background block as Settings, Widgets right there, and the card on the shelf follows each change. - **A card pops up when a reminder comes due, with quick actions, and quick to-dos can have a reminder time**: when a reminder, a water reminder or a to-do comes due, a dark glass card 380pt wide slides out under the notch. On the left is the character with a matching mood, the speaker in the top corner names that kind's sound (crossed out when it is set to Off, sounds are off, or Do Not Disturb, a Focus or screen sharing silences it this time), the first line says which kind and when, the big line is what to do, the third line is the time and where it came from, below are any others due at the same time, and the quick actions sit bottom right. ## Install

1. Download the latest `.dmg` or `.zip` from the [Releases page](../../releases)
   and move `Dockling.app` to Applications.
2. **The build is not signed or notarized**, so macOS 26 warns the first time you open it:
   - Double-click it, then close the warning;
   - Open **System Settings > Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway** next to Dockling;
   - Authenticate and confirm.

   Or remove the quarantine attribute in Terminal:

   ```sh
   xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Dockling.app
   ```

3. **Look in the menu bar on first launch.** Dockling is an accessory app with no Dock icon and no main window.
   Settings, the widget library and layout switching live in the menu bar icon.

### Known limitations

- An ad-hoc signed accessory app gets no location prompt from macOS, so enter a city for the Weather widget in **Settings > Widgets**.
- Now Playing needs Dockling allowed under **Privacy & Security > Automation**; reading browser tabs also needs "Allow JavaScript from Apple Events" in the browser's Develop menu.
- Lunar dates, solar terms, the "Chinese" clock face and the End User License Agreement stay in Chinese in the English interface.

## Build from source

You need macOS 26 and Xcode. The Xcode project is generated from `project.yml` and is not in the repository:

```sh
brew install xcodegen
xcodegen generate
xcodebuild -scheme Docket -configuration Release build
```

Interface text lives in String Catalogs under `Resources/Localization`. Simplified Chinese is the source language and English is the translation. At runtime the development region in Info.plist is English, so languages Dockling does not ship fall back to English, and the Traditional Chinese and Hong Kong tables carry the Simplified text.

## How the screenshots are made

Every image in this README is taken by the screenshot tool in this repository: `Tools/Shots` puts the app's real shelf and panels in real windows on screen, and `scripts/capture-shots.sh` captures them with `screencapture`. They are not mockups and no number is edited by hand.

Run `scripts/capture-shots.sh` for the Chinese interface (written to `docs/images/zh`) or `SHOTS_LANG=en scripts/capture-shots.sh` for English (written to `docs/images/en`). It needs Screen Recording permission for your terminal and takes a few minutes. The **Actions > Screenshots** workflow takes both sets on a GitHub macOS runner and uploads them as an artifact.
